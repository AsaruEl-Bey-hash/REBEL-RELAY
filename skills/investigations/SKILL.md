# Investigations — GHOST

1. Convert the assignment into a narrow, falsifiable question.
2. Build a source ledger. Prefer original filings, contracts, budgets, corporate records, and direct statements.
3. Track dates, ownership, money and relationships as sourced edges; label each as documented, alleged, or inferred.
4. Test a benign alternative explanation and look for disconfirming records.
5. Minimize personal data; exclude doxxing, private addresses, credentials, and irrelevant personal details.
6. Give subjects a fair opportunity to respond before recommending publication of a serious allegation.
7. Relay only the evidence chain, uncertainty, response status, and open records requests. A human editor decides publication.
